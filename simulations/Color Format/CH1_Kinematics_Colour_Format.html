Kinematics Colour Format

Eight tokens, six tone families. Every arrow, line, shaded area and number in a kinematics simulation takes the colour of the quantity it represents — never the colour of the graph it happens to sit on.

v1.08 tokens6 themes in use: Motion & Kinematics Lab v1.4.0

The six families — what a colour means before you read the label

how far

Distance and displacement. Metres. The green pair separates the scalar (deeper) from the vector (lighter).

how fast

Speed and velocity. Metres per second. Same scalar/vector split, same logic.

rate of change

Acceleration only. How fast the “how fast” is changing.

construction

Not a quantity — a line you draw on a graph to extract one. Tangents, rise-over-run steps, and the labels that name them.

the axis

Time. Desaturated on purpose: time is the stage every other quantity is measured against, never the subject.

the apparatus

A number read directly off the equipment before any physics is done with it.

Within the green and blue pairs the scalar is the deeper shade and the vector the lighter one. That is the whole distance/displacement and speed/velocity distinction, carried by tone rather than by a legend a student has to memorise.

Shaded areas

An area under a graph is not a new quantity — it is the quantity it evaluates to. So it keeps that quantity's colour and only drops in opacity, far enough that the gridlines and any curve crossing it stay readable. Two levels, shared by every mode:

var AREA_A  = 0.11;   /* the standard fill: area under v–t, ut rectangles, tape bars */
var AREA_A2 = 0.13;   /* the second component, when two areas are stacked or adjacent */

fillArea(fr.v, mod.v, withA(C.s, AREA_A), 0, tc);        /* area under v–t ⇒ displacement */
shadeRect(g, 0, q.t, 0, q.u, withA(C.v, AREA_A));        /* ut       ⇒ the u part of s     */
shadeTri (g, [[0,q.u],[q.t,q.u],[q.t,q.v]], withA(C.a, AREA_A2)); /* ½at² ⇒ the a part of s */

When a total area is built from several parts, each part keeps its own quantity colour — that is what makes s = ut + ½at² legible as a picture rather than a formula. Every fill is clipped to its frame, so a shaded region can never bleed into the panel below.

Rules

  1. Colour by quantity, not by graph

    Velocity is blue whether it is an arrow on the road, a line on the v–t plot, a dot, or a number in the side panel. A student who learns one code reads all four modes.

  2. The construction colour is never a quantity

    Orange marks the act of measuring a gradient — the dashed tangent, the rise/run step, the “gradient = …” caption, and the panel rows that report a gradient. The result of that construction is reported in the quantity's own colour beside it.

  3. Time stays desaturated

    Grey for the time axis, the hover cursor, and Δt. Time is the stage, not an actor; if time competes for attention the graph stops reading.

  4. Gold means “straight off the apparatus”

    A tape gap, a card width, a ramp angle — a number the student physically reads before doing any physics with it. Once it becomes a velocity it turns blue.

  5. u and v share one colour

    They are the same quantity at two instants. The difference is carried by the marker, not the hue: a hollow dot for the start, a solid dot for the end.

  6. Structural greys are not tokens

    The moving object, the bench, the ruler and the frame are drawn in the theme's own text/border greys. Reserve the eight tokens for things that carry physics.

  7. No token may collide inside a theme

    Two quantities sharing a hex in any one theme is a bug, not a style choice — it silently teaches that two different things are the same thing.

Conformance — what a new theme has to satisfy

A theme is only valid if each token lands in its family's band, measured as HSL on the hex:

  • greenhue 80–170
  • bluehue 175–265
  • redhue ≥325 or ≤15
  • orangehue 16–36
  • goldhue 40–60
  • greysaturation < 0.28

Both checks run in the UI suite: every quantity keeps its tone family in all 6 themes and no two quantities ever share a colour, in any theme. Add a theme, run the suite — a hue that drifted out of its family fails by name, with the offending hex.

Extending past kinematics

Kinematics claims six families and leaves the rest of the wheel free. When CH1 continues into dynamics, keep these eight fixed and take new tokens from unclaimed hue space — violet/magenta for force and its components, teal for mass and inertia, and a second gold-adjacent tone only if the apparatus genuinely reads something new. A quantity that appears in two chapters keeps the colour it was given here: momentum is built from velocity, so it should live in the blue family rather than start a seventh one.
